# Hypothesis Verdict

**Date:** 2026-10-27
**Status:** Tested. Verdict: **partly supported**.

## Hypothesis

At Risk + Cannot Lose Them buyers are a better retention target per contact than Hibernating buyers. Hibernating is the largest segment, but part of its size may come from the one-month data window.

## What would have overturned it

| Condition | What I found | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Hibernating shows similar or higher recoverable revenue per contact | At a 5% response rate, At Risk + Cannot Lose Them earn 21.05 per contact against 15.03 for Hibernating (about 1.4 times higher, from average order values of 420.94 and 300.53). Hibernating would need about a 7% response rate to match. | Not overturned, but the margin is modest |
| At Risk buyers show little recent browsing | 41.1% of At Risk + Cannot Lose Them viewed or carted since Oct 18, against 38.1% of Hibernating. Carting: 5.0% against 3.4%. About 59% of At Risk + Cannot Lose Them were silent. | Not overturned, only weak support |
| Their funnel drop-off is no worse than other segments | Segments contain only buyers, so funnel drop-off cannot be measured by segment. As a proxy, about 78% of At Risk + Cannot Lose Them bought through the cart (against about 50% of Hibernating), so cart-based outreach can reach them. | Not directly testable |

## Second lever: cart abandoners

134,320 users added an item to the cart and never purchased in October. The average cart item price is 324.07. If 2%, 5%, or 10% of them were won back, each buying one item at the average price, recovered revenue would be about 0.87M, 2.18M, or 4.35M. Per contact at 5%, this is about 16.20, between At Risk + Cannot Lose Them (21.05) and Hibernating (15.03).

## Verdict: partly supported

None of the conditions that would overturn the hypothesis was triggered. But the evidence is thin: the value advantage is modest and a small difference in response rate could erase it, the recent-activity advantage is small, and one condition could not be tested directly. In total, Hibernating holds more recoverable revenue (about 2.34M against 0.27M at 5%) because it has about 12 times more buyers, so the advantage applies only under a limited contact budget.

## Limitations

- The dataset has no contact or response data, so the 2%, 5%, and 10% response rates are assumptions, and the same rate is applied to every group.
- Revenue is gross revenue, not profit. Discounts and the cost of contacting buyers are not included.
- Everything comes from one month (October 2019).
- Some Hibernating buyers may return without being contacted, so not all recovered revenue would be incremental.
- The hypothesis was formed from the same data used to check it, so this is exploratory evidence.

## Next step

Run an A/B test. Randomly split a segment into a contacted group and a control group, compare repeat purchases within 14 days, and measure the real response rate and the cost per contact.
